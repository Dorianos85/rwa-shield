See ECV View on GitHub

Problem

Oracle price is not cash in the pool.

Fixed-LTV lending on tokenized equities assumes you can exit at the mark. On Solana, after hours and on weekends, the book is thin, the base market is closed, and a liquidation that looks solvent on paper becomes bad debt in the pool.

Fixed LTV

Marks collateral at oracle × constant haircut. Ignores depth, session state, and realized volatility. Weekend gaps turn “healthy” books into ~$4M losses in our stress scenarios.

Executable value

Asks what USDC the route actually returns under current liquidity and calendar. Refuses new credit when recovery would fail — without zeroing existing collateral marks.

Product

ECV: collateral valued as recoverable USDC.

Six terms. Pure functions. Same model for live pricing, backtests, and the agent swarm. The breaker gates new borrows — it never cascade-liquidates an open book.

ECV = Poracle × D(q) × S(q) × M(t) × V(σ)   ·   B = breaker
P
Oracle

Mark from the price feed — the starting point, not the answer.

D
Depth

How much of the book can absorb this size without collapsing.

S
Slippage

Route impact on Jupiter (or offline fallback) for this notional.

M
Session

NYSE open / after-hours / weekend / holiday — when the hedge exists.

V
Volatility

Haircut from realized vol so stress is priced before it hits.

B
Breaker

Stops new credit when feeds fail. Leaves open positions marked.

Proof

Same book. Two models. One weekend.

110 days of hourly synthetic SPYx-style data. Positions every 7h, 96h maturity, session-aware liquidation delay. Highlight: weekend gap −22% + thin book.

Weekend tail · gap −22% · thin book

Fixed LTV keeps lending into a closed market and a shallow route. When the gap opens, the pool eats ~$3.98M of bad debt.

ECV refuses the credit beforehand. Calm markets: ECV lends ~the same as fixed LTV. Stress: denial of credit — not loss of the pool.
